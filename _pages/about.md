I am a PhD candidate at [Stevens Institute of Technology](https://www.stevens.edu), working with [Prof. Kaijian Liu](https://researchwith.stevens.edu/en/persons/kaijian-liu/). I develop computer vision and deep learning methods that help robots perceive and act in visually degraded environments, using underwater infrastructure inspection. Underwater images suffer from light scattering, color distortion, and low light, and labeled field data is scarce, so models that perform well on standard benchmarks often break down in the field.

My research addresses this along four lines:

- **Robust perception:** combining underwater image enhancement with semantic segmentation to locate bridge components in low-visibility inspection images ([_Automation in Construction_, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autcon.2025.106230)).
- **Learning from limited data:** knowledge-guided few-shot segmentation of multi-class damage from a small number of field images.
- **Efficient deployment:** structured pruning to make deep vision models smaller and faster for real-world use (Runner-up Paper Award, IConIC 2026).
- **Robot autonomy:** ontologies that let underwater robots reason about maintenance tasks, structural damage, and environmental conditions.

My earlier work applied machine learning to geotechnical engineering problems, such as predicting soil liquefaction and strength. I received an MS in civil engineering from Imam Khomeini International University and am a recipient of the Provost Doctoral Fellowship at Stevens.

**I will complete my PhD in May 2027 and am seeking tenure-track faculty and industry R&D positions in computer vision and robotic perception.**
